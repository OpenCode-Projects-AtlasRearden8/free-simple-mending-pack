Sewing Kit Starter Checklist

Everything a beginner actually needs is here — two punchy pages of shopping and a supply list that fits in one tin. Check each box as you tick it off in the store.

The full kit (grab these first)

  • Sewing scissors — a small pair dedicated to fabric (don't use them on paper; paper dulls them)
  • Hand needles — one variety pack of sharps, sizes 5–10
  • All-purpose thread — two spools: one black, one white, plus one in the color you wear most
  • Heavy-duty thread — for denim, bags, and anything that takes strain
  • Straight pins + a little tin or wrist pincushion to keep them in (magnets love pins, and so do bare feet if you don't)
  • Needle threader — a couple cents of wire that saves a half hour now and then. They break; buy two.
  • Measuring tape — soft, flexible, sewist's style
  • Thimble — one that fits the middle finger of your sewing hand snugly
  • Seam ripper — for unpicking stitches; it is the most-used tool in my drawer
  • Safety pins — a small mixed pack; they also handle fallen-hem rescues
  • Beeswax or thread wax — run thread through it once to stop knots and tangles

Nice to have (second visit)

  • Iron-on patch assortment — knees and elbows will thank you
  • Iron and ironing board or pressing mat — if you don't already own one, a mini steam iron works fine
  • Fabric scraps bag — anything 4 inches or bigger, keep in a shoebox for patching
  • Wonder Clips or binder clips — hold layers without poking you
  • Fray-check liquid — seals raw edges without sewing (great for fallen hems)
  • Regular dyed buttons — a $3 jar of spare buttons saves any button pop-off
  • Storage tin or box — any flat tin will do. If it lives in one predictable place, you'll actually mend things.
Kind tips: A sewing starter kit is cheap. It's not a hobby budget; it's about the same as one pair of pants. Once you own it, every fix after that costs thread and elbow grease.
