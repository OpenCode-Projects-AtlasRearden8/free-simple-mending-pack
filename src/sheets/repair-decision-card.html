Repair or Replace?

A calm two-question card to keep by your mending basket. It won't tell you what to do — it just makes the decision quicker and kinder.

Question 2: Would the work be more than the item is worth?

Try this quick math instead of agonizing:

  1. Time it saves: if the fix takes under 20 minutes, the “worth” hurdle is almost always cleared. Most of our mends live here.
  2. Heart it costs: if it's a favorite, a gift, or hard to replace in your color, add that to the value side. Sentiment counts. Favorites usually save you a shopping trip you'd rather skip anyway.
  3. Cost it saves: a tailor's hem costs about the same as a pizza. Sewing it yourself tonight costs a bit of thread and one episode of your show.
  4. Burden it shrinks: a drawer of “maybe someday” broken things is a mental tax. Pick the best two, fix those, and release the rest without guilt.
The 20-minute rule: if you can finish it before dinner, do it now while the tea's hot. If you can't, schedule it — a mend that waits a week is a mend that usually waits a year.

Keep this card

Tape it inside a closet door or slide it into your sewing kit lid. The point isn't to be efficient — it's to stop dreading the little pile.

When in doubt

Fix it. A slightly wonky mend on a comfortable, loved thing beats a brand-new replacement you had to go out and shop for. Wonky mends soften with wash and wear.

Kind tips: Release items with gratitude and without guilt. If you've never missed it, it was never in the way.
